A working MLE's guide to SOTA generative models

Published on September, 2026

I recently (September 2026) gave a talk on SOTA Generative Models at work. These are my companion notes for that presentation. I am unable to share the content of the slides here. However, the material covered in this article is designed to be self-sufficient.

A few words about the scope

I keep the scope limited to understanding models that generate sample for various modalities in a non-autoregressive way. While autoregressive models are getting better, flow/diffusion-based models are still (at the time of writing) considered state-of-the-art (SOTA) across various modalities such as images, videos, or protein structure synthesis.

I deliberately do not cover neural architecture of these models here. This choice is made primarily due to 2 reasons - (a) it's not architectural innovations that made these models SOTA; it was the mathematical insight that unified flow/diffusion models under a single framework, making training these models impressively easier and (b) the architecture part of these models should be familiar to MLEs, even with a minimal working knowledge about the transformer architecture, with perhaps one exception about variational autoencoders (VAEs), which, I believe deserves a separate article on their own right.

At the time of writing, most of the SOTA models across modalities (images: Nano Banana Pro, videos: MiniMax H3, protein systhesis: AlphaFold 3) use the flow/diffusion framing established by the stochastic interpolants formulation. Gathering an intuitive understanding of how these models work would be the primary focus of this article.

A few words for people who know DDPM

People who are familiar with original diffusion models (e.g., DDPM) might find a few things presented differently (e.g., there are no forward or reverse process). However, the new framework makes this more sound pedagogically. A brief callout to earlier diffusion models is made in chapter 4.
