A working MLE's guide to SOTA generative models

I recently (September 2026) gave a talk on SOTA Generative Models. These are my companion notes for that presentation. I am unable to share the content of the slides here due to various reasons. However, the material covered here is designed to be self-sufficient.

A few words about the scope

I keep the scope limited to understanding models that generate sample for various modalities in a non-autoregressive way. While autoregressive models are getting better, at the time of this write-up, flow/diffusion-based models are still considered state-of-the-art (SOTA) across various modalities such as images, videos, or protein structure synthesis.

We deliberately do not cover neural architecture of these models here. This choice is made primarily due to 2 reasons - (a) it's not architectural innovations that made these models SOTA, rather it was the mathematical insight that unified flow/diffusion models under a single framework, making training these models impressively easier and (b) the architecture part of these models should be familiar to anyone with a minimal working knowledge about the transformer architecture, with perhaps one exception about variolational autoencoders, which, I believe deserves a separate article on their own rights.

At the time of writing, most of the SOTA models across modalities (images: nano-banana pro, videos: minimax H3, protein systhesis: alphafold 3) use the flow/diffusion setup established by the stochastic interpolants framework. This would be the primary focus of this article.

A few words for people who know DDPM

People who are familiar with original diffusion models (e.g., DDPM) might find a few things presented differently (e.g., there are no forward or reverse process). However, the new framework makes this more sound pedagogically. A brief callout to earlier diffusion models is made in chapter 4.
