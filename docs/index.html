A working MLE's guide to SOTA generative models

I recently (September 2026) gave a talk on SOTA Generative Models. These are my companion notes for the presentation. I am unable to host the content of the slides due to various reasons. However, the material covered here is designed to be self-sufficient.

A few words about the scope

We keep the scope limited to models that generates samples for various modalities in a non-autoregressive way. While autoregressive models are getting better, at the moment of this write-up, flow/diffusion-based models are still considered state-of-the-art (SOTA) across varioius modalities such as images, videos, or protein synthesis. We deliberately do not cover neural architecture of these models - as I believe it's not architectural innovations that made these models SOTA, rather it's the mathematical framework that unifies flow/diffusion models under the same framework that made training these models impressively easier. At the time of writing, most of the SOTA models across modalities (images: nano-banana pro, videos: minimax H3, protein systhesis: alphafold 3) use the flow/diffusion setup established by the stochastic interpolants framework.

People who are familiar with original diffusion models (e.g., DDPM) might find a few things presented differently (e.g., there are no forward or reverse process). However, the new framework makes this more sound pedagogically. A brief callout to earlier diffusion models is made in chapter 4.
